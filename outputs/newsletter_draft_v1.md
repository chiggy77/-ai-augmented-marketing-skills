Subject: What 8,466 inbound leads tell us about where to focus our follow-up and sales effort this quarter
We looked at 8,466 inbound leads from a public lead-scoring dataset to see where conversion really comes from. It was a game-changer for how we think about follow-up, because the signal is much clearer than we expected and it is not in the channel report we usually read.

The first finding is about time on site. Leads who spent more than 10 minutes on the website converted at 67%, while leads who spent up to 10 minutes converted at only 15%, which means that the single most useful way to rank leads is simply to look at how long they stayed. We should route the first group to sales on the same day and leave the second group to automated nurturing.

The second finding is about channels. Google and organic search convert at 40% and 38%, direct traffic at 32%, and chat leads at only 25%, so chat is a source we should improve before we spend more on it.

The third finding is a warning. Leads that bounced our email converted at 5%, and leads that asked not to be emailed converted at 12%, so list hygiene is worth fixing now.
